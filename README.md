# Togle

Togle is free and open source, under the [MIT license](LICENSE). It runs on your computer and uses an AI plan you already have: your ChatGPT plan (through OpenAI's [Sign in with ChatGPT](#sign-in-with-chatgpt)), a Claude subscription, or an API key.

**Togle Microphone**, a separate app (not open source), removes background noise from your voice in every call app. Togle works without it, and finds it by itself when it's installed. See [Togle Microphone](#togle-microphone).

An AI assistant for any video call, on Windows and macOS, with Linux packaging available. See [macOS setup and testing](docs/macos.md) and [Ubuntu setup and testing](docs/linux.md). Togle listens to your voice and video calls (Zoom, Meet, Teams, WhatsApp, anything that plays through your speakers), transcribes both sides, and suggests what to say when someone asks you something.

**Optional recent dictation history.** In Settings → Dictate anywhere → Recent dictations, turn on Keep recent text for the selected account. Togle keeps the latest 50 completed dictations for seven days, encrypted on this device, including recognized words and the final text. Expired entries are removed while the app runs and on the next startup. Correct text in history, then explicitly choose Remember to keep a short spelling correction in that account’s vocabulary. History edits do not type into another app. Turning history off or clearing it invalidates pending saves from older recordings. Audio is never retained.

**Automatic, editable meeting notes.** Saved calls generate their own notes after ending, including when the agent’s learning is off. Generation uses the call’s original subscription account and pauses for live calls or dictation. Open the call’s Overview to edit notes, choose Concise, Detailed, or Action items, and add personal observations. Your edits are protected from late model replies and learning reviews. Regenerating edited notes requires a replacement choice. Personal notes are included in exports and the next generation. Settings → Privacy → Automatic meeting notes controls background generation. Long transcripts are processed in complete bounded chunks rather than silently truncated.

**Words and spoken shortcuts.** In Settings → Dictate anywhere → Words & shortcuts, save names, product terms, and recurring recognition corrections. A spoken phrase such as “my work email” inserts its saved text exactly. These entries are encrypted on this device, separate for each dictation account, and frozen for each recording. Explicit corrections and shortcuts also work with cleanup off or when cleanup fails. Standalone shortcuts skip text-model inference; embedded shortcut contents are kept out of the cleanup prompt. Vocabulary and shortcuts do not retain audio. Text history is a separate, opt-in setting.

**Ask about a saved call.** Open an agent’s Calls, select a recording, and use Ask about this call, Action items, or Draft follow-up. The answer streams in that agent’s existing chat through its selected AI account. It can read the original saved transcript even when the call has no summary, with timestamps, tentative speaker names, and explicit paging for long calls. Selected-call questions use read-only archive tools. Chat can also search that agent’s saved transcripts across meetings; another agent’s calls stay outside that scope. These features add no Togle subscription or usage quota; the selected AI provider’s limits still apply.

It isn't made for one kind of call. What it knows and how it helps come from its **agents**: you **train** one for your calls by talking to it, giving it material and trying it on questions (it learns from each call too), or you get one from the **agent market** (lessons, a clinic's front desk, interviews, sales, support, one-on-ones…) and train it from there. The first agent, General, is for any call.

**Use an AI plan you already have.** Togle currently connects to ChatGPT and to Claude through the native Agent SDK, with API-key and compatible-server options available. Answers and speech-to-text are configured separately: local speech keeps audio processing on this PC after a one-time download. Provider plan limits and eligibility still apply. See [subscription integration boundaries](docs/product-architecture.md#subscription-integrations) before distributing a product that offers these sign-ins.

**Dictate into a text box.** On Windows, hold **F9** while speaking, then release it to enter your words into the focused chat, editor or terminal. **Esc** cancels. Settings → Dictate anywhere also offers press-to-start/stop behavior and custom shortcuts. While idle or speaking, the voice indicator is a tiny 48 × 12 bar that ripples with microphone audio. After release, a compact spinner shows **Transcribing → Cleaning up → Inserting**, followed by **Inserted**. Connection setup or a slow destination has its own label. Hover to reveal the shortcut, status and settings without taking keyboard focus. While idle the microphone is off. Hide the idle bar in Settings or the tray menu. A blocked shortcut is shown on hover and in Settings; clicking the bar still starts and stops dictation.

**Personal and work accounts.** Add separately signed-in Claude or ChatGPT accounts under Settings → AI for answers. Pick one global default; an agent can override it under Agent settings → Knowledge → AI account. Its chat, calls, document study and learning use that account. Live calls pin their account until the call is cleared; changing the sidebar or default account cannot reroute them. Missing or signed-out overrides report an error rather than fall back. ChatGPT profiles have separate OS-encrypted token files; additional Claude profiles use separate official-runtime configuration and secure-storage namespaces. Existing sign-ins remain available. Account ids are omitted from shared agent packages. A display name supplied by the subscription identifies the user in prompts and the People list, with explicit user corrections taking precedence.

**Togle stays in the tray.** Closing the main window keeps dictation and any active call running. Click the tray icon to reopen Togle, or right-click for voice-bar visibility, dictation controls, settings, and **Quit Togle**. Quitting during a call or dictation asks first. Dictation never presses Send or Enter; if the original field or cursor changes, the bar keeps the text for Copy.

**Build and open the current app.** `npm run pack` cleans generated `out` and `dist` files, builds the app, and verifies the packaged code and resources against a build manifest. On Windows the canonical executable is `dist/win-unpacked/Togle.exe`; `npm run open` opens that verified copy, and `npm run open -- --shortcut` updates the Windows Start Menu entry to it. A different running copy must be quit before opening this one. `dist/build-info.json` identifies the version, source fingerprint, commit and build time. `npm run dist` creates installers through the same clean pipeline. `npm run clean` removes only generated `out` and `dist` files; app settings, accounts, call history and downloaded speech models are kept. Packaging refuses stale build inputs or a running app in its output directory. Custom output directories must be empty or carry this checkout's build marker before they can be cleaned.

Dictation opens the microphone while speech setup and text-box detection run in parallel. Early audio waits in a bounded queue, and a slow text box does not block recognition or cleanup. Claude audio streams during recording in roughly 128 ms batches over one account-pinned connection. The cleanup SDK process also prepares while you speak, without sending a prompt until the transcript is final. Release drains the microphone's final partial frame before closing the stream. Cancellation closes both resources and prevents late text from reaching another field. Choose a headset in **Settings → Dictate anywhere → Microphone**; device changes apply to your next dictation and never switch the microphone mid-recording. A paused source or no incoming sound turns the indicator amber; hover for microphone settings. The bar stays small while transcription and cleanup finish automatically. If insertion fails, hover the amber bar to recover the text with **Copy text**. Collapsing the bar or a failed copy keeps your words available to retry.

**Dictation account** defaults to the global subscription, with an optional separate profile for dictation. **Claude** sends audio to the same account-backed speech service as Claude Code; with **ChatGPT**, this PC's speech recognition hears it (Sign in with ChatGPT doesn't include transcription), and ChatGPT cleans it up. Dictation never silently falls back to another provider. Cleanup stays on the same account, with an eight-second deadline and the original words retained on failure. Turn cleanup off for the fastest result. Call transcription remains a separate setting and can still run locally.

Claude speech is a compatibility integration with Claude Code 2.1.312's WebSocket protocol, not a public Agent SDK transcription method. The official SDK handles sign-in refresh and organization policy. Only the main process reads its current access token from Claude Code's account storage (the keychain on macOS); Togle does not copy credentials, write refresh tokens, or expose them to the renderer. Account, workspace and language are checked when opening and stay pinned for that connection. Prepared cleanup rechecks the pinned identity before sending the final transcript. Voice-disabled organization policy is respected. Interim updates replace each other, utterance endpoints append sentences, and completion waits for the stream to close normally so later sentences are not lost. Aborted, rejected or broken streams never insert a partial result.

Automatic insertion supports accessible Windows text fields, rich editors and console/terminal input. Hold mode captures the destination at release; toggle mode keeps the starting destination. Togle rechecks the focused window and field before insertion, and checks contents and selection where the app exposes them. Terminals and editors without reliable value access receive Unicode keyboard input once; delivery is acknowledged, but the resulting text cannot always be verified. Togle never presses Enter, submits a message, executes a command or changes the clipboard. Changed or unsupported targets, password fields and multiline formatting keep the result for **Copy text**. **Copy last text** recovers the last result until Togle quits; raw audio and dictation history are not saved. During a call, overlapping microphone dictation is excluded from Togle's transcript; mute the conferencing app's microphone yourself for private dictation.

Dictation checks: `npm run test:dictation:ui` exercises the production bar, repeated hover, audio feedback, idle/recording/completion, and recovery with a fake microphone; `npm run test:dictation:hover` moves the real Windows pointer across its own fixture to verify stable hover, retained keyboard focus, and clicks through the transparent area (leave the mouse still; the test restores its position and stops if you move it); `npm run test:tray` exercises the real app lifecycle, menus and speech selection with an isolated profile and a shell-icon double; `npm run test:dictation:native` types only into its own Windows fixture and checks actual F9 release through the production bar, audio flush, controller and native insertion, including writable combo boxes and rich editors without forced accessibility (desktop must be unlocked; speech results are scripted); `npm run test:dictation:live` checks a known recording, repeated sentences, silence and cancellation through Claude's speech service and subscription cleanup, with local recognition and ChatGPT disabled. `test/claude-speech.test.mjs` covers authentication refresh, account/workspace changes, organization restrictions, framing, finalization, server failures, cancellation and a real local WebSocket server.

`npm run test:dictation:ui -- --native` checks the visible overlay and its always-on-top behavior without moving the desktop pointer or typing into another app. `npm run test:dictation:latency` plays the same six-second fixture at microphone speed through the previous batch/cold-cleanup path and twice through live streaming/prepared cleanup. It uses the signed-in Claude subscription, no microphone, and a simulated destination; `test/.work/dictation-latency/result.json` separates release-to-text-ready, transcription, and cleanup times. These timings exclude native insertion and vary with service load and network conditions. `-- --cleanup-only` measures cleanup separately with known text.

**Choose an agent on the left.** Each agent has **Chat**, **Live Call** and **Call History**, with a visible selected tab and keyboard navigation. Start a call from Chat or Live Call; setup offers optional notes and a screen or audio-only choice. Live Call contains the running transcript and suggestions, while Call History lists only that agent?s saved calls. All three tabs remain available while inspecting a saved call. A call keeps its owner while you browse another agent; Live Call and the running-call indicator return to it. Agent settings holds profile, knowledge, skills and learning. The [product architecture](docs/product-architecture.md) records these boundaries.

**Knowledge and skills belong to each agent.** Under Agent settings, **Knowledge** holds short facts, reference documents, workflow descriptions learned from calls, and approved answers. **Skills** describes how Togle should help during calls: what to suggest, verify or clarify, and when to stay quiet. Earlier call-derived procedures remain intact as reference knowledge and are retrieved when relevant. New assistance skills require real correction evidence before automatic activation; unsupported proposals remain in Learning for review. An answer becomes approved only through your approval or correction. Facts and skills retain their source call or user edit, and skills have revisions. Context for this answer shows the facts, assistance policies and reference material supplied to a response, without claiming the model used every item. Learning offers Undo. Manual edits use no model request; stale edits cannot overwrite newer learning.

**Readable replies and notes.** Chats, live answers and saved notes support clickable web/email links, headings, lists, tables, quotes and code blocks with Copy. Links open in the browser or mail app. Streamed replies defer formatting behind typing and preserve completed blocks. HTML stays literal text; embedded images appear as links without downloading them. `npm run test:ui` checks compact and wide layouts, link destinations, exact code copying and streaming responsiveness with isolated fixtures; timings are saved in `test/.work/ui/rich-text-performance.json`.

**Useful call names.** History uses the linked calendar meeting name, including when you start manually during one unambiguous meeting on the agent’s calendar. Preparation notes and resumed stretches retain that meeting identity. Otherwise, meeting notes or review supply a concise topic title. Older greeting-based titles become dated call labels and are named quietly from their saved content while Togle is idle. Naming uses the call’s saved AI account/profile, pauses for calls and F9, caches results and backs off after a connection failure. Names you edit yourself are preserved. `npm run test:call-titles:live` checks fictional English and French meetings through the signed-in Claude subscription.

**Teach the agent your words.** Say or type “When I say north wind traitors, it means Northwind Traders” or “When I say so three, write SOH 3.” Explicit corrections belong to that agent, apply to its call transcripts and dictation, and appear under Knowledge → Words and names and Learning → Undo. Clear wording lessons need no model request. Automatic call learning runs quietly; pending approvals and saved changes remain inspectable. The **Mic** and **Call** meters pause Togle’s two listening inputs independently. These controls change what Togle hears; use your meeting app to mute what other participants hear.

The idle voice bar is a translucent 48×12 CSS-pixel pill with a live waveform while speaking. Hover for 500 ms to reveal controls; drag the bar to move it. Placement survives renderer recovery and app restarts. Recording and processing keep the same native window, with readable progress until insertion finishes. [Speaker changes, agent wording, and verification](docs/speech-learning.md).

**Automatic screen context:** Start call asks which screen or window Togle can watch, with an audio-only option. A timestamped local buffer lets the AI request a still or interval from when someone spoke, even if its decision comes later. “As you can see on my screen” marks that original audio interval. Expand the screen timing details to inspect requested and actual capture times. Pause or change the source from the call controls. [How screen context works](docs/screen-context.md).

Learning can improve an agent automatically, ask before remembering, or run only when requested. Ordinary reusable additions can be kept automatically; prices, deals, and changes to existing knowledge wait for approval. Each call shows what was kept, proposals awaiting approval, and Undo. Creating an agent from an included template uses no model request and starts with its own empty memory. Older calls retain the summaries saved by previous versions; full transcript history is available for new calls when Remember calls is enabled.

## How it works

**Meetings and live controls.** Clicking the agent currently on a call returns to the live workspace. The title bar also offers **Return to live call** from other views. Each suggestion has its own Collapse/Expand control, remembered while navigating. **Group on/off** can change during the call; multiple matched voices or explicit group greetings offer group mode, and a paused automatic assistant offers to turn suggestions on.

**Calendar reminders.** Settings → Meeting reminders reads a Calendar connector connected to the signed-in Claude account and offers assistance five minutes before a scheduled meeting, with an agent choice. Calendar attendee counts preselect group mode. Checks use the Claude plan every 15 minutes while idle and are cancelled during a call; cached events drive local reminders. No microphone or screen capture begins until Start assistance and the source choice. Connecting a calendar to Codex or sharing only free/busy does not provide Togle meeting titles/attendees. Calendar access must be connected in Claude; unavailable access is shown instead of invented meetings.

**Speaker labels.** Local [speaker embeddings](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/speaker-identification/) and [neural speaker segmentation](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/speaker-diarization/index.html) run on one CPU thread in a separate process, alongside whichever speech provider you use. Verified 28 MB multilingual and 6 MB segmentation models download once. The latter checks for speaker changes and overlapping speech before identity matching; audio flagged as mixed is excluded from training. Quiet audio is normalized, and only detected speech enters the voice encoder. New people need repeated evidence or sustained clean speech; one short clip cannot create another person. Known voices use several samples, competing remembered identities and turn timing, so changes in volume or delivery are less likely to split one person into several labels. Consecutive chunks from the same speaker display as one turn in the live transcript, history, and export; original chunk timestamps remain available for screen context. **Group on/off** uses the same control as **Auto on/off**, beside it.

Explicit introductions suggest a name with `?`. **People** lets you confirm a name or combine duplicate labels using **Same person as…**, including in a saved call's Transcript. With **Settings → Speakers → Remember confirmed people** enabled (the default), confirming a name during a call saves a local, OS-encrypted voice profile after enough clear speech. It can recognize that person in later calls and after reopening Togle. No recordings enter voice memory, and recognition uses no cloud service or subscription quota. Unconfirmed voices remain temporary. This setting is independent of retaining call history; turn memory off to stop using it, or **Forget** a person to remove their saved profile. Names already in transcripts remain.

Each remembered person keeps several confirmed acoustic references, including variations you explicitly confirm through **Choose remembered person** when their microphone changes. Similar names never imply the same person. Automatic guesses do not train saved profiles, and correcting a guess does not rename the other person's identity. Familiar voices can be checked after 1.5 seconds of speech, then at most every three additional seconds; short replies need a stronger match. Repeated prefixes cannot enroll a person. A completed mixed turn withdraws an early label. Brief uncertain replies can be relabeled after a clear matching turn within the next 90 seconds, without being used as training samples. Strong evidence can identify a different familiar person immediately despite turn continuity, even before they have previously spoken in this call. Only four clips wait for the worker, independently of transcription and replies. Ambiguous or mixed turns can stay unassigned; detection is imperfect and does not separate simultaneous voices into individual recordings.

`npm run test:voices:live` checks three real recorded speakers, changed volume/filtering/noise, and speaker changes at the beginning, middle and end of long clips. `npm run test:voices:memory` downloads 128 public [LibriSpeech clean/test recordings](https://huggingface.co/datasets/openslr/librispeech_asr) from 16 people (CC-BY-4.0; ffmpeg required for initial decoding). Through the actual Electron worker and OS encryption, it enrolls 12 people on separate utterances, restores their identities from disk, then checks 60 held-out familiar and 20 unfamiliar turns, early captions, five-person calls, mixed speech, opt-out and forgetting. Reports are under `test/.work/voice-benchmark/`; no meeting audio is used. `npm run test:voices:benchmark` also compares three encoders using the current CAM++ thresholds; other models need separate calibration before those scores can be compared fairly. These controlled recordings and the five-person synthetic regression are regression checks, not a general meeting accuracy benchmark.

`npm run test:voices:meetings` adds 336 annotated clips from two public [AMI meetings](https://groups.inf.ed.ac.uk/ami/download/) with four people each (CC-BY-4.0; about 100 MB of fixtures). Checksums and fixed enrollment clips are in `test/voice-meetings-reference.json`. The earlier implementation gave a single label to 94 of 123 mixed-speaker clips; the current version does so on 24. On 155 clearly single-speaker clips, correct remembered names changed from 127 to 121, with zero wrong names in either run; the others remain unidentified. Matching prioritizes avoiding false attribution over filling every label. Clip boundaries come from manual annotations, so this measures identity recognition and mixed-turn rejection, not end-to-end meeting diarization error. Reports are under `test/.work/voice-meetings/`. Neither these meetings nor the read-speech fixtures establish human-level performance or guaranteed accuracy for your calls.

**Changed-screen snapshots.** During selected-source monitoring, meaningful visual changes also retain local timeline snapshots, at most one every two seconds. These do not trigger a background AI request. The two-frame-per-second rolling buffer and original capture timestamps remain available for exact historical lookups. Mouse movement alone is ignored; scrolls, slides and edits are picked up by visual change detection. Pause or end the call to stop capture.

**Claude connected apps:** In **Agent settings → Knowledge → Connected apps**, select apps already connected to your Claude account. With Claude subscription selected in AI settings, the agent can search and read them in chat and during calls. App selections belong to that agent and Claude account. Togle checks each tool's read-only declaration and blocks writes, unknown tools, local MCP commands, and tools from other sources. Connection metadata is cached; each answer rechecks the actual connected tools. This does not sync Claude web or Cowork conversation history. Run `npm run test:claude-connections:live` for an opt-in check that performs real Slack channel search and Atlassian resource discovery without opening messages or issues.

During a call, **Auto on/off** pauses or resumes automatic replies while transcription continues. **Suggest reply** is always available. The labeled **Mic** and **Call audio** meters show which inputs are connected; **Reconnect audio** retries a missing input without ending the call or restarting the working side. A new default device is picked up automatically.

- **Me:** your microphone. **Them:** the PC's audio output, captured with WASAPI loopback on Windows, Electron's Core Audio tap on macOS (14.2 and later; macOS asks first), and on Linux the PulseAudio or PipeWire monitor of the speakers, which Chromium lists as an input. No virtual audio driver is needed.
- Voice activity detection cuts each stream into utterances on this PC, and each finished one is transcribed by the option picked under **Transcribe with** in Settings → Speech to text: the ChatGPT plan's dictation unless you pick another, or this PC's own speech recognition (GPU when there is one), with which audio never leaves the PC (below).
- **This PC's speech recognition is installed only if you pick it**, not with Togle: with This PC as the speech model, Settings → Speech to text offers it (the window says so until it's there). It downloads whisper.cpp's own build for the system (release b5454: CUDA on Windows with an NVIDIA GPU, else the CPU's; the CPU's on Linux, x64 and arm64; Metal on macOS) from GitHub, and the models from Hugging Face: Whisper large-v3-turbo (574 MB) on a GPU or base (148 MB) on a CPU, and Parakeet (669 MB, for English). With an NVIDIA GPU that's 1.9 GB in all. Files come as short range requests over 8 connections (a host throttles a long one), an install stopped or cut off picks up where it was, and every file's SHA-256 is checked before anything in it runs. A file this PC has already (a development copy from `npm run setup:whisper`, or the source's copy for a build run from `dist`) isn't downloaded: checked the same way, it's linked in, and when all of it is there on the same drive, Togle takes it as it starts. They're kept out of the roaming profile on Windows (`%LOCALAPPDATA%\togle\speech`), in `~/.local/share/togle/speech` on Linux and Togle's own folder on macOS, and **Remove** deletes them. macOS's build has no `whisper-server`, so there Whisper runs from the library itself in a utility process of its own (`whisper-worker.ts`), set up as the server sets itself up and given the same 16-bit samples the server reads from a WAV file: run that way on Windows, it got exactly the server's word errors in `npm run speech` (0, 2.0, 4.5 and 8.5% from a clean line to chatter 5 dB below), in about 95 ms an utterance rather than 130-150. Fed raw float samples instead, one noisy question came out "Zap" for "SAP".
- **This PC's transcription is tuned for accented English** (Indian, Eastern European, Spanish, Nigerian, Chinese…), including weak pronunciation. Measured on real accented conversations (EdAcc) and poorly pronounced speech (speechocean762), word errors and missed content words:

  | Recognizer | Accented conversation: content missed | Very poor pronunciation: content missed | Average over six test sets |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Whisper large-v3-turbo (English) | 9.5–15.4% | 27.7% | 15.0% |
  | Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 | 8.0–13.8% | 32.8% | 14.5% |
  | **Parakeet, Whisper for unclear speech (Togle)** | 7.6–13.8% | 29.9% | **14.0%** |

  So NVIDIA's Parakeet (through whisper.cpp's `parakeet.dll`, about 40 ms per utterance on a GPU, in its own process) transcribes everything, including live captions. When it's unsure (mean token confidence under 0.8) or switches language, Whisper large-v3-turbo redoes that utterance: locked to English, with beam search and the agent's **Words to recognize** as hints. On a noisy line Whisper takes the utterance from the start, because there Parakeet drops words while still sounding sure: with background chatter 5 dB below the caller, "Does it work with SAP?" came out as "Does it work?", and the words wrong fell from 15.5% to 8.5% with Whisper (`npm run speech`). That costs about 100 ms per utterance, only on noisy lines; live captions stay with Parakeet. Transcription is English-only, so heavily accented English is never mistaken for another language.
- **What hears the call.** Settings → Speech to text → **Transcribe with** is one choice, for your voice and other people's alike: this PC (all of the above; where a new Togle starts, and what a ChatGPT account uses, since Sign in with ChatGPT doesn't include transcription), Claude's speech with a Claude subscription, OpenAI (`gpt-transcribe`, with your OpenAI API key, billed by the minute of speech and apart from the ChatGPT plan), or another server with OpenAI's speech API (`/v1/audio/transcriptions`): a model you run yourself with speaches, LocalAI or vLLM, or a service such as Groq. With Claude or an API, finished utterances go there, each with the call's language and the agent's words to spell (as gpt-transcribe's keywords, or the prompt Whisper takes); live captions and answers started at a pause come from this PC's models when they're installed, and without them there are none: a line shows once it's heard, and its answer starts then. A line the API couldn't hear (the server down, no network, a language it turned down, which it's then asked without) is heard on this PC instead when its models are there, and the window says why; a key it turns down stops the requests until there's another, and "too many requests" until the wait it gives is over. Claude Code has [voice dictation](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/voice-dictation) with a Claude.ai sign-in, but the Agent SDK does not expose its microphone dictation or accept Togle's recorded call audio. For Claude answers in Togle, pick this PC or a speech API to transcribe the call.
- **A ChatGPT account's speech.** OpenAI's Sign in with ChatGPT covers text and images, not audio or transcription, so with a ChatGPT account Togle hears your calls and your voice typing on this PC: nothing is uploaded to be transcribed. ChatGPT still answers, and cleans up dictation when that's on.
- Audio goes through a proper anti-aliasing filter (96-tap FIR) before the 16 kHz conversion, and utterances are cut at pauses, never mid-word: past 15 s at a 250 ms pause, at most 28 s at the quietest moment.
- **No headphones?** The mic also picks up the other side. Togle drops "Me" lines that repeat what they said at the same moment (all 9 echo lines in a test call, none of yours). Headphones are still better.
- Unplugging a headset or switching the default mic or speakers reopens capture on the new device. If Whisper or Parakeet crashes or hangs, it restarts and the utterance is retried.
- When the other side asks a question, Togle sends the recent transcript plus your notes about the call to the model and streams back a reply you can say. You can also ask on demand, attach a screenshot of the screen under your cursor, or get a summary.
- **Help when a reply is still needed.** Automatic suggestions can stay quiet for rhetorical or self-answered questions, questions meant for someone else, and a conversation that has moved on. Their cards wait for that decision as well as the final transcript. A pending suggestion is discarded if you answer before it appears, including any speculative deep answer; a brief listening cue such as "I see" keeps the question open. Suggest still gives you help when you ask for it. `npm run perf` counts discarded work separately from answers shown, without recording the call's words.
- **Answers start at the pause.** Deciding that someone has finished takes 600 ms of silence, and auto-suggest waited 300 ms more after a question. Now, when the other side pauses for a quarter of a second after something ending in "?", Togle transcribes it right away (tens of milliseconds on a GPU) and starts the answer out of sight. The answer shows the moment the end-of-speech check confirms the same words, and is dropped unseen if they go on talking; a click or a breath after the question doesn't count as talking (heard again, it's the same words), so the answer under way goes on rather than being asked again. A question they follow up on in the same breath waits for that answer, which covers both lines, instead of getting a card of its own. The model starts about 0.65 s sooner.
- **Facts from your documents at once.** When a question ends, Togle searches the agent's documents on this PC (milliseconds) and shows the lines that answer it, such as "Growth: €39 per user per month", while the model is still writing: in the end-to-end test they appeared about 0.7 s after the caller's last word, two seconds before the answer's first words. Only lines that share the question's key words make it, cut to the sentences of them that do, never a question; and an approved answer for the question takes their place. What you told the agent since that answers it comes first, with when ("you told me yesterday 17:40"), so a document that says otherwise isn't all you see. Of a work session, what the AI said and its tools found count, not your own requests in it: measured on the hard and week scenarios, 8 of the 19 lines shown were such echoes, headers and titles, and after this 1 of 21 was off.
- **Two speeds.** Most questions want an answer now; a few need real thinking. Every answer starts with a quick one from a fast model, which also judges the question. When it's hard (designing a system, debugging, code, trade-offs), a stronger model's deep answer streams in below while you're already talking. Questions that are clearly debugging or design from their wording ("how would you design…", a stack trace) start the deep answer at once instead of waiting for that verdict; everyday wording like "How do we…?" waits for it, since starting the strong model on 11 of 15 everyday questions only to drop it a second later cost a request each time. A question pointing at the screen ("why does this fail?") starts it early only when the screen shows code (an editor, a terminal, a stack trace, a file of code): not for a slide that mentions "payment methods", a "QR code" or an "error rate", where 30 of 30 everyday questions had started the strong model for nothing. One started early that a newer question overtakes stops with its card's quick answer. **Think deeper** (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+D, or the button on a card) asks for it any time, and asking again thinks harder (up to ChatGPT's xhigh, or Claude's max); the key deepens the newest card unless they've asked something since (an "Okay." isn't asking). A summary of the call goes on while the next question gets its suggestion. "A crash course on your pricing", "a mistake of 450 euros on the invoice" or a bug bounty isn't debugging ("greška 500", "a 500 error" or "error 404" is), and statements that want an answer ("I'd like to know how you price it.", "I'm curious about the roadmap.", "Zanima me…", "Pitam se…") get one like questions do, and so do requests to a group or with "Please" ("Tell us about yourself.", "Please walk me through the setup."), while "Give me a second.", "Tell me if you need anything.", "Da, zanima nas." or "I'm curious too." don't start one.
- If an approved answer matches the question, it shows instantly, before any model replies. When that question is all they asked ("Does it work with SAP?" for "Do you integrate with SAP?"), no model is asked at all: it would only repeat it. Anything more in the turn ("and with Salesforce?"), asking when, where, why or who ("When will it work with SAP?"), a question that needs thinking through, or a suggestion asked with a screenshot gets a model answer too.
- **Pair programming.** Give an agent your project folders (the paperclip → **A project's code…**) and its deep answers search and read the real code (read-only, through `git grep`/`git ls-files` when the folder is a repo) before answering, citing files and lines, with fixes as code blocks you can copy. When the code isn't on your PC (someone else shares their screen), frames are kept at up to 2560 px: at that size 6.1 Sol transcribed small code from a 4K screen exactly, where 1600 px gave misread characters. The deep answer gets the key frames in full; quick answers and frame descriptions get 1600 px to stay fast. You can also paste an error or a stack trace into the box to write in during the call.
- **Long, messy calls.** **New call** stops what the call before still had running in the background (describing its screen, its notes), so the new call's first snapshot is described at once. Once a call outgrows what fits in a prompt, Togle keeps running notes of the older part in the background: decisions, numbers, who's who, what you already answered, and in debugging sessions what was tried and what it showed. Answers late in a long call still know what was settled early. Auto-suggest skips "can you hear me?"-type checks, and answers every part of a multi-part question.
- **Poor connections.** Requests go through Chromium's network stack (Electron `net.fetch`), which got 16 of 16 requests through on a lossy connection where Node's `fetch` lost 5. A request that gets no response in 12 s is retried, and so is one that fails before any text arrives. A stream that goes silent for 25 s after its text started is closed, so it can't hang. When the deep answer starts on a hunch, the quick one gets a head start, because parallel requests on a slow link finish one after another.
- **A second copy for a slow answer.** Now and then a request waits on a slow server: in the tests most quick answers started writing within 2.5 s, and even in a slow stretch within 4 s, but a few took 4.5–7.5 s. A quick answer that hasn't written anything after 4.5 s gets a second copy of its request, and whichever writes first is kept (the other is stopped). It costs a request only for those outliers, and not for one already at work (looking something up, or saying what it's doing), which a copy would only follow.
- **Your plan's limits.** With a ChatGPT account, Togle shows **Using ChatGPT plan · Manage usage** beside the call's tools; ChatGPT's settings show what Togle used of your plan, and let you set a limit for it. When a limit is reached, the notice says so, with **Manage usage**. For a few minutes Togle doesn't answer, describe the screen or take notes on its own, then tries again; **Suggest** still tries meanwhile, and when an API model is set up the notice offers it.
- Each job has its own model and reasoning effort (Settings). The defaults come from measuring speed and answer quality on the ChatGPT plan's models:

  | Job | Default | Why |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | Quick answers | GPT-6 Luna, low | Writes ~3× faster than the others; correct on concept questions |
  | Deep answers & code | GPT-6.1 Sol, medium | Covered every point on design and debugging questions, and caught extra risks in code |
  | Reading the screen | GPT-6.1 Sol, low | Read every arrow of a dense architecture diagram; Luna missed one |
  | Background work | GPT-6 Luna, low | Summaries, studying documents, the call review |

  Each job can also use its model's **Fast** tier where the plan has one (ChatGPT's Fast mode). It writes about 1.5 times as fast: deep answers finished in 22–23 s instead of 28–37 s. Quick answers are too short to gain from it (the first words came after 2.35 s against 2.40 s), and for the deep model the plan says it uses more of your limits, so it's off by default.
- **The call as a timeline.** Togle treats a video call like a movie: one shared screen, several people talking. Each utterance is tied to the frame that was on screen while it was said.
  - **Watch it as the call goes** (in the **Screen** menu, or Ctrl+Alt+Shift+R) follows a screen or one window (e.g. the Zoom window). It checks it 10 times a second on a 320×180 grid and saves every visible change with its time, once the picture holds still for 0.4 s: one frame where a slide transition or a scroll ended, not several on the way there. While something keeps changing (typing), every 2 s.
  - Changes are compared against the last *saved* frame, so a diagram drawn stroke by stroke or text typed letter by letter still gets saved as it grows.
  - Places that never keep still, like people on camera or a playing video, are left out of that comparison, and so is the restless area right around them (shoulders, hands). Typing or a new bullet right next to a strip of video tiles still counts, and so does typing in a pane that scrolled a moment before, once it stops. On recorded calls this kept 2.5–23× fewer frames (a 12-minute speaker view: 239 instead of 594; 44 minutes of slides beside the presenter's video: 133 instead of 3,089), without missing a slide or a scrolled document.
  - A change away from anything in motion, like a stack trace pasted into the meeting chat beside the video, is new content: it's always described (right after snapshots, however many other frames came with it; those that don't fit a round wait for the next), and goes with the next answer before it is, even when the rest of the picture shows the same people as before. Each frame is stamped with when its change began, so what's said during a slide transition is tied to the new slide.
  - **Save a picture of it for the call** (in the **Screen** menu, or Ctrl+Alt+Shift+S) saves one picture on purpose.
  - With more than one screen, **Suggest from the screen** and **Save a picture** ask which screen, with a small picture of each (Enter takes the one you picked last). Their hotkeys take the screen under the mouse, so pointing at a screen picks it. Snapshots are taken at the screen's full pixels, up to 2560 wide, also on a screen scaled to 150%.
  - In the background, the model describes new frames in batches (snapshots first, at most one request every 10 s). For diagrams it lists components, labeled arrows with direction, and what was just added. It also says when a frame shows only people on camera, or nothing new. While that's all it sees, it waits twice as long each round, up to a minute. A new frame that looks like people it already described (a tiny 32×18 copy is compared) needs no description at all, and one that looks like nothing described yet, such as a screen share starting, brings it back to every 10 s. Simulated on the same recorded calls with a question every 30 s, description requests went from 209–353 an hour to 83–144.
  - Each answer gets the timeline plus frame images. The deep answer gets up to 5 frames that matter: the one on screen when the question was asked and the newest (both in full), and others from the last few minutes. The quick answer gets only those key frames, at 1600 px: with five frames, one in full, its request came to 2.7 MB, which has to upload before the model even starts; now it's under 1 MB. Older frames reach answers through their descriptions, and a frame's prepared image is reused from one answer to the next. Frames of only people go with no answer, and frames with nothing new stay out of the timeline.
  - Frames are saved under `%APPDATA%\togle\sessions` and deleted after 3 days (or on New call). Togle hides itself from capture while recording.
- **A brief for the call.** Tell the agent who the call is with and what you want from it ("Discovery call with Northwind's head of support at 3; I want a two-week pilot") and it sets the call's brief, shown next to its name, and says what earlier calls with them said. Every answer, the running notes and the call review read it, ahead of the call itself; asked "So what would you suggest as a next step?", the quick answer proposed that pilot. It's saved with the call, and a new call clears it, unless you told it about the new one. It's only for the call that's next: about to start, under way, or at a time you give for today. Told about one without a time ("for the Globex call: …") or on another day ("for tomorrow's standup"), the agent sets no brief, which would be on whichever call came first. What you said about that call still reaches it, with the conversation (see Talking to it).
- **Group calls.** Tell the agent when several people are on the call ("a few of their team will join"), and set your name in Settings → Suggestions ("Luka, Luke"). Then a question to you by name ("Luka, what's your take?") gets a suggestion, one to someone else by name ("Marko, can you share the numbers?", or "Marko, quick question." before it) doesn't, and one that names nobody does when it goes on from your exchange: right after you spoke (an "Okay.", a "Thanks, Luka." or a "Mhm." while you talked between still counts), or right after a question of theirs to you, also one you pressed Suggest for (they asked two in a row). A question between that someone else answered starts their exchange. Someone else is said to only by a first name people have: about 5,100 of them, from the US Social Security Administration's baby names (public domain) and the common names of Croatia and its neighbours (also as Croatian calls them: "Ivane", "Majo", "Ivice"), the rest of Europe, Turkey, the Middle East, India and East Asia; or by any name after a title ("Herr Müller, …"). So a topic, a product or a word that opens a question ("Security, how do you…?", "Debezium, is that what you use?", "Still, …", "Usput, …", "Jasno.", "Rok, …") is no one, nor are names that are mostly words or places ("Summer", "Chase", "Paris", "Sydney"). Names closing a question count ("Could you share your screen, Marko?"), several at once count together ("Ana and Luka, …", "Ana, Marko, and Luka, …", "Hi Ana, hi Luka, …", "Marko or Luka, …"), and so does "Question for Luka: …"; names that are introduced or among the speakers don't ("Ana, our CFO, has a question…", "Marko, Ana and I reviewed your proposal…"). Your own name is trusted more: without commas, as speech recognition often writes it ("Luka what do you think?", "What about you Luka?"; not "Luka said…"), in a sentence of its own ("Thanks, Luka. What about the timeline?"), after a title ("Gospodine Dutina, …", "Mr Dutina, …"), hyphenated ("Ana-Marija"), in the Croatian forms it's called by ("Ivane", "Petre", "Majo" for Ivan, Petar, Maja), and whatever it looks like ("Kimberly", "April"), but not when you're asked about ("Did you talk to Luka?", "Is this for Luka?"). A full name in Settings ("Luka Dutina") answers to either part, and your names go last in what speech recognition listens for, which keeps them when the list is long. When your own line comes in late (transcribed again for being unclear) and shows their question followed it, the suggestion comes then, unless you already replied or pressed Suggest. Suggest still answers anything on demand. In a call between two, every question gets its suggestion as before.
- **The call is never lost.** What was said, the running notes and the suggestions are saved as the call goes (2 s after a change, at least every 10 s), encrypted with Windows' DPAPI like the ChatGPT sign-in, in `%APPDATA%\togle\call.bin`. Close Togle, or have it crash, and the next start picks the call up again, as a stretch of the conversation (**Learn from this call** under it, when there's something for the call review to learn from); a brief told before the call, or a question typed before anyone spoke, is kept too. Picking a call up doesn't make it newer: it's still dated from its last change. When Windows shuts down mid-save, the call is written at once and again after that save; one cleared with **New call** just then stays cleared. A save that fails (a full disk, the file held by another program) is tried again, and closing tries it once more. **New call** also stops the questions being asked again, and what was said or snapshotted before it and comes in late stays with the call before. Frames still waiting for a description are described when the call is picked up (not the ones left to their neighbours). A new call deletes it, and a call older than 3 days isn't picked up. If the window itself fails, **Reload** brings the call back as it was (an answer still streaming goes on) and closing doesn't wait for a question it can't show; reloading also stops what the page was running (listening, recording).

## The window

Togle floats over the call, and is one conversation with your agent, as Hermes Agent is one chat: you talk to it before, during and after calls, and nothing needs filling in or ticking. It opens where you left it, at the size you left it (back where it first opens if that screen is gone).

- **Your agent** is the name in the title bar. Its menu switches agents, starts a **New call**, or opens **Manage agents** and **Browse agents**. Sharing and deleting are in each agent's menu under Manage agents.
- **The conversation**, kept across restarts: what you tell it and its replies, each change it made with **Undo**; each call, with the suggestions it brought (Approve and Correct are gone: it learns from what you actually said, and from what you tell it), folded to a line once the next call begins; and after each call what it learned, with Undo, asking in words about what it held back, with **quick replies** a click away ("Keep them", "Don't keep them", "Keep notes"), or tell it what's right. Each suggestion shows what they said that it answers, so with questions coming fast, or looking back, it's clear which answer is for what; and then what to say, with **Think deeper** and **Copy** quietly under it. A saved answer that fits shows first, as **Your answer**. The ones before it in the call are a line each (the start of the answer; what they asked, when you point at it), a click opens one, so the newest is what the eye lands on: measured with three suggestions, the call takes 168 px of the window instead of 354. Once a call is over, its stretch offers its **Summary**, **What was said** (and shown, with **Copy** for your notes), and **Learn from this call** when there's something to learn from. Once it goes back before today, a line marks where each day begins. Scrolled up to read, a button goes back to the newest, and says **New suggestion** (or message) when one came in meanwhile, so a suggestion is never missed below.
- **During a call**, above the message box: audio levels, the latest transcript, **Suggest reply**, and screen context controls. The selected source and AI reading activity stay visible; **Pause screen**, **Resume screen**, and **Change** control monitoring. **Call tools** keeps manual snapshots, screen-assisted replies, and the summary available. Click the latest transcript to expand it.
- **At the bottom, the box to write in:** during a call it asks about the call; otherwise it talks to the agent. In it, the paperclip (files, a folder or a project's code for it to learn from or read; or drop them into the conversation); beside it, **Start call**, after which the box is ready to type in (so Enter asks, and never presses **End call**). Started again within two minutes of stopping, it's the same call going on; later, a new call, and the call before is looked back at first if it hadn't been.
- **Before the first call**, connect an AI and choose transcription. **Set up call** opens the missing step. With Claude connected, **Set up on this PC** selects local transcription and opens its installer; downloading starts only when you click **Install**.
- **Settings** separates **AI for answers** from **Speech to text**. Model options, group-call details, and **Window & shortcuts** stay folded until needed. Changes save automatically. Escape returns to the conversation.

From the title bar:

- **Keep on top**: on by default, in Settings → Window & shortcuts → Window, with the window opacity (30–100%).
- **Quit** (✕): during a call (listening or recording) it asks first, and so does Alt+F4; the call is saved either way.
- **Hidden from capture** (the crossed-out eye beside the call's clock): screenshots, screen recordings and screen sharing show what's behind Togle, so only you see it. It's on by default (Settings → Privacy → Hide from screen sharing turns it off) and needs Windows 10 version 2004 or later; modern macOS apps using ScreenCaptureKit can still capture Togle despite this setting, and Linux has no capture exclusion (Settings explains both). Togle's dropdown lists and tooltips are separate windows, and they're hidden too, since Electron's own protection misses them. Togle applies this at startup and whenever you change a window setting; it doesn't keep re-checking afterwards.

The window stays light, so the call app keeps the PC's time: each entry of the conversation is drawn again only when it changes (with 500 entries kept, a key pressed during a call takes 1.5 ms of the window's time instead of 17, a caption 1.4 ms instead of 14); with nothing happening on a call it's busy 4–6 ms a second instead of 30–35, since the pulsing dot and the levels are drawn by the GPU; an hour of what was said unfolded costs a caption 3 ms; and its script is minified (320 kB instead of 755). A conversation opens with its latest 60 entries drawn, and earlier ones come in as you scroll up to them, what you were reading staying where it was: 500 entries open in 45 ms with nothing held up, where drawing them all froze the window for 55. Togle's main process is ready about 75 ms sooner too, as the Anthropic SDK and JSZip load the first time they're needed rather than on every start.

## Agents

An agent is yours to shape for any kind of call: lessons you teach, a clinic's front desk, podcast interviews, sales, one-on-ones. You pick one for each call and train it for whatever your calls need; Togle itself has no kind of call built in. It learns the way [Hermes Agent](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent) does: you talk to it, it learns from each call on its own, it tells you what it learned, and every change can be undone. Its training changes in two places only: what you tell it in the conversation, and the **call review** at the end of a call, which it tells there too. The agent's name at the top of the window opens its menu: pick the agent to talk to and for the call, **All agents** (yours, each with what it was taught: "3 skills · 2 answers · 12 facts · 4 documents"), or **Agent market**.

**Training one.** Its conversation is where it's trained. A new one opens on the ways to: things to tell it (a click puts one in the box, to change first), the paperclip for documents, a website or your AI chats, and **Try a question**, also beside the paperclip: write what someone might ask on a call, and the agent answers it exactly as a call would (its approved answers first, its documents, its skills), as a card in the conversation. Not right? Say what it should be ("No, say we can only move it to Thursday"): its model reads the question tried and its answer, and keeps yours as the approved answer to that question, with **Undo**. **Try again** on the card asks it once more, and asked just what it was taught, the answer is yours, at once, with no model asked. A question tried isn't something you told it: calls don't read it as that. Measured on the ChatGPT plan (2026-10-28, `npm run scenarios -- --only talk:try-say+try-wrong`): after it tried "Do you have a discount for nonprofits?" and made one up, both "No, say we don't have a nonprofit discount, but yearly billing gives two months free" and "That's wrong, there's no nonprofit discount… offer that instead" kept the right answer to that question (the second kept the fact as well), and nothing kept the made-up discount.

**The agent market.** **Agents → Market** (or **Agent market** in the agent's menu, or the welcome of a new conversation) lists agents for kinds of calls, by category (Business, Work, Health, Teaching, Career, Media) or searched, each with what it brings (its category, how many skills and answers) and **Installed** once you have it. Thirteen come with Togle, each with skills of its own: Sales Call, Customer Support, Investor Pitch, a Zagreb real estate agency's calls (in Croatian), Team Meeting, Engineering 1:1 Coach, Hiring Interviewer, Dental Front Desk, Doctor's Visit (as the patient), Spanish Tutor, Math Tutor, Interview Practice and Founder Podcast Host. They're `hub-template/agents`, read from the app's own folder, through the same checks and limits as a hub on GitHub, and with no connection. **More sources** adds a hub on GitHub by its `owner/repo` (its agents are listed with them, saying which hub they're from), and **Install it from a file…** takes a `.togle` file. Whichever way it comes, an agent is shown in full before it's installed, and installed it's an agent of your own, to train on your calls.

**Making one.** **New agent** asks what calls it's for. Say it in a sentence or two, in any language ("Guitar lessons I give online, mostly to beginners"), and one request sets it up: its name, what it's for, its instructions, two to four skills for the moments those calls bring, and the words speech recognition should spell right, never inventing a price, a policy or a fact the description didn't give. It's the one you talk to from then on, and its conversation starts with what you said and each change it made, each with **Undo**; tell it more from there. Below the box: get one ready-made from the market, or start blank. The use cases that were templates (sales, support, team meeting, investor pitch, interviewing, interview practice) are agents of the market now, with skills.

**Sharing one, and the Hub.** Open **Agent settings → Share agent**. It goes as a folder of plain files: `AGENT.md` (its setup), each skill as a `SKILL.md` in the open [agentskills.io](https://agentskills.io/specification) format that Hermes Agent and Claude use too, and its approved answers. Memory facts and documents go only if you tick them, none ticked to begin with; what you told it about yourself, your conversation with it, its earlier calls, your work sessions and its history never go. **Save as a file** writes a `.togle` file to send to anyone; once you've added a hub, **Publish to** it writes the folder and opens the hub's GitHub page for adding it, which makes a pull request for the hub's keepers to review. The market lists the agents of each hub you added under **More sources** (a hub is a GitHub repository of agent folders with an `index.json`, read from `raw.githubusercontent.com`, as Hermes Agent's skill taps are) beside the ones that come with Togle. Whichever way it comes, an agent is checked and shown before it's installed (its documents by how each starts, and whether you have this same version already). In what the agent is (its instructions, skills, answers, memory, and its documents' names), words that try to override Togle's instructions, send what's said on a call to a web address or an email address, build a link from it, ask for passwords, codes or payment details, ask to be paid in gift cards or crypto, or hide text from the reader (characters that show text in another order than it's read, invisible tag characters, an HTML comment) block the install; a "never…" before them doesn't ("Never ask for their password"), and Persian, Arabic and emoji text is fine. In a document, reference material, the same are shown to look at. Links, email addresses, phone numbers and pictures it may suggest are listed too, and so is how many requests of your plan studying its documents will take, when they're long. A package is read within an agent's sizes before anything else (a small file that unzips to gigabytes, or a hub file that never ends, is refused at once), and installing is all or nothing. Installed, it's a new agent of your own, whatever you have already; nothing in a package is ever run. `hub-template/` is a ready hub: the five agents drafted below, `scripts/build-index.mjs` (builds `index.json` and checks each agent as Togle will), and workflows that check every pull request and rebuild the index on merge.

**How it gets better.** The same loop as Hermes Agent's, fitted to calls (measured on the real model below):
- **It learns from what you say, carefully.** What you tell it in the conversation is yours: kept as you said it ("that answer about SSO was wrong, we support SAML on Scale"). The call review sets each of Togle's suggestions beside what you said after it, and keeps a plain, full answer you gave to a question that will come up again. What you said is weighed, not trusted, since speech recognition mishears and what you tell one customer may be for them alone:
  - From what you said alone it keeps no guess ("I think", "let me check"), no price, discount, deal or date you offered (that stays with the call), no rule about wording or length (only your corrections teach those), and no reply that didn't answer what they asked.
  - It only adds on its own. A change to what it knows (a fact rewritten or forgotten, a rule of a skill reworded) waits for your yes, as Hermes Agent's background review stages its changes and removals of memory, and so does an amount or deal heard on the call that it doesn't already have. It asks about those in the conversation ("I haven't kept these yet…"): **Keep them**, **Don't keep them**, or say which are right ("only the HubSpot one", "it's 39, not 53"), and it keeps just that.
- **Skills improve in place.** A skill the call called for is the one the review improves: what it said stays, a reworded rule takes the place of the one it rewords, and new rules join it. A new skill is named for the kind of situation, not for the call.
- **Two kinds of memory.** Facts true on every call (your product, prices, terms) stay in saved memory. Live answers select up to 4,000 relevant characters, and can retrieve other saved facts when needed. Approving a fact does not require deleting another one to fit that prompt budget. Facts about the people on a call (their company, size, tools, deadlines) stay with that call: Togle keeps a summary of each reviewed call, encrypted on this PC (Settings → Privacy → Remember calls turns it off), and brings it back on the next call with the same people. The review names who each call was with (their company, the people by full name), and a later call finds it when its brief, or someone on it, names that company or a person by first and last name together: a tool or a city both calls mention, a first name or a surname alone, or your own company's name isn't enough. Told about the call, the agent says what the earlier one said; found once, it stays for the rest of the call, shown at the top of the call in the conversation.
- **It counts what it uses.** Each approved answer shown on a call and each skill a moment calls for is counted, and written when the call ends; tidying up sees how much each is used.
- **A call that teaches it becomes a document.** A training or a walkthrough (a colleague explaining the API, a new feature, how to troubleshoot) can contain more detail than a short fact should hold. When the review sees a call taught you about your product, company or work, the agent offers to keep notes of it: **Keep notes**, or a yes, and one more request writes the whole call up as notes by topic (every fact, number, setting and step taught, a way to check something as numbered steps, and what the screens showed), kept in its Documents, where every later call looks up what a question needs. Nothing beyond the call's summary is kept unless you say, **Undo** takes it out again, and a call with a customer is never offered as one. A way of handling something a colleague taught on the call (the steps to check) becomes a skill.
- **It tidies up.** At most weekly, after a call review and once it has learned enough for things to overlap (never during a call), it merges skills for the same kind of situation, drops an answer to a question it already answers, and merges related facts. A skill you wrote or taught can take others in but isn't merged away. Each change can be undone, and "tidy up your training" does it now, saying what it merged, each with Undo. Measured (2026-10-28, `--only tidy`, 1 request): three skills learned for pushback on price, one named "Acme's discount question", became one "Pricing objections" with all four of their rules; a skill for security questions kept its two rules, and a free-trial question answered twice and the Scale price said twice were each kept once.

**Talking to it.** Tell it in your own words what it should know or do ("when someone says we're too expensive, ask what they compare us with first", "keep suggestions to two sentences", "our Scale plan is now 35 euros"). It changes its own training (a skill, an approved answer, a fact, its instructions, About me or words to recognize) and shows each change in the conversation, with **Undo** next to it. It can read the call open in Togle (what was said and shown, what each suggestion said), so "the answer about SSO on the call was wrong, we support SAML on Scale" fixes that answer. It hears what each call review said and held back, so "yes, but only the first" after one keeps just that, and it sets the call's brief when you tell it about a call. What you tell it goes with your calls too: every answer on a call reads the last week of this conversation (its newest 6,000 characters, each message with when it was said, and when calls were), as words from you rather than from the call. So "for tomorrow's standup: if they ask about Android, it's Marko's team, and they'll know by Friday" is there when they ask, without being saved as training. What you write to it during a call (Stop, write, and Start again: it's the same call going on) is marked as written then, so "don't give them dates" on a finance call isn't taken for the standup after it; and what you undid since (an amount it misheard and remembered) is said to be undone. Older notes come back as well, from the last month, so notes for a call a week ahead don't depend on how much you pasted since:
- what you wrote that names who or what the call is about (its brief or what's said on it, as earlier calls are found: "Initech" brings back the notes for the Initech call), after the call's timeline;
- what you wrote that answers the question being asked, right beside it with its day. Where your word is newer than a document or a work session, it holds over it: "we keep Safari 16 until March, Marta changed her mind", said yesterday, beat a session from three weeks before that said it would be dropped.

Names you wrote to it in the last week (who the coming calls are with) are also passed to speech recognition, even ones said only once. What was said on the calls themselves stays out of it, and a question tried in the chat doesn't get it. A fact that changed is updated in place rather than added twice (one named exactly is the one meant, also when another contains it or it's there twice), and an edit of the instructions keeps their lines apart. It can search the agent's documents, for example to turn a sales guide into skills. Material you paste (a price list, an FAQ) goes into its documents, without the model copying it out, and so does a web page you give the address of (only one you wrote), or the whole site when you ask for all of it (the pages it links to in that section, up to 20). A long paste goes back to the model with later messages cut to its start; one over 40,000 characters (a whole chat from claude.ai or ChatGPT) goes even the first time by its start and its end, enough to tell what it is, while saving it as a document takes all of it. In the conversation, a message of yours over 1,500 characters shows folded, with **Show all**. Told to use a work session ("the Claude Code session from yesterday about the checkout crash") or a chat you exported from claude.ai or ChatGPT, it finds it among yours and adds it, asking which when more than one could be it. Ask it how it would answer a question, and it says. Needs a model that can call tools: the ChatGPT subscription, a Claude API key, or most OpenAI-compatible models (see Setup).

**The conversation is kept**, as Hermes Agent keeps its sessions: each agent's, across restarts, encrypted on this PC (DPAPI), with Undo still on every line it changed; each call in it with its suggestions (its latest 40 calls; not when summaries of past calls are off in Settings, when a call stays a line), and the latest 500 entries, newest first, as much as a conversation may hold (6 million characters): a log pasted in is kept by its first 50,000, and when it would come to more, the oldest entries and the oldest calls' suggestions make room, so a conversation that grew too big is never left unsaved. What was said before goes back to its model, so it picks up where you left off. Deleting the agent deletes it.

**What it knows is asked, not looked up.** There's no page of it to manage: ask ("what do you know about our pricing?", "what do you remember about Northwind?"), and tell it to change or forget anything ("remove the old price list", "forget the calls with Northwind", "tidy up your training"). Forgetting a document or an earlier call can't be undone; everything else it changes can. Behind that, the agent has:
- **Documents:** attached with the paperclip beside the box (files, or a folder: its first 500 files), or dropped into the conversation: PDF, Word, PowerPoint (with speaker notes), text (also UTF-16, as Excel's "Unicode Text" and Windows PowerShell write it, and text that isn't Unicode, as Excel's CSV is, in the code page it was saved in), Markdown, CSV, JSON, HTML (the text it shows, not its markup); pasted text; and web pages (read in a hidden sandboxed Chromium window so JavaScript-rendered docs work). It says what went in, each with Undo. A file or page added again after it changed replaces its document (and is studied again); unchanged, it says so. A document added during a call is in the next answer, and the same files added twice at once go in once. One whose studying failed for a reason that passes (not signed in yet, no connection, the plan's limit) is studied again at the next start or sign-in.
- **Work sessions:** your sessions with Claude Code and Codex on this PC (in the terminal, an editor or their apps), for calls about that work: the paperclip's **An AI chat or coding session…** lists them, newest first, with a search by what it was about or its folder; or tell it ("use my Codex session about the payment retries"), or drop a session's file in. What goes in is what was said, day by day; a line for what the AI did (files edited, commands run, but not ones that only read); what a tool of your workspace found for it (a Jira ticket, a GitHub issue: up to 1,500 characters of each); your answers to its questions; and the latest summary the tool wrote of the session. What tools printed, the AI's own reasoning and its helper agents' work stay out, and keys, tokens and passwords anywhere in it are taken out. It's studied at once, however small, for where the work stands (one background request), and that shows under the line that says it went in, with **Undo**. It goes in under the tool's title and, once studied, is named by what it's about, unless you named it yourself in Claude Code (`/rename`): Claude Code titles many sessions just "Codebase", or not at all, and Codex gives several the same name. Two of one name get their day, then a number, so a line never points at the wrong one. Before each call, a session you've worked on since is read again and studied again; what it was studied for stays until that's done (and if studying it fails, it's tried again before the next call). Not for a call started again within five minutes, and not for a new title alone; one you take out meanwhile stays out, and one the tool has deleted since stays as it was read. A first request with a picture or a long log pasted in is found too, a command run with words after it ("/fix-issue SSO login fails") is what you asked, and a copy of the folder kept beside it (`~/.claude.bak`) never takes the place of the one in use. Use Claude Code or Codex with more than one account (a folder for each, as `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR=~/.claude-work` or a second `CODEX_HOME` makes), and each one's sessions are found, named by its folder: "Claude Code", "Claude Code (work)", "Codex (acme)". Folders beside the one in use, named after it, count when they hold sessions, so another tool's `~/.claude-server-commander` doesn't. Each agent can have its own, one or several: tell it ("this agent is for my job at Acme: use my work Claude Code"), or pick them at the top of that list (**Look in:** **All**, or its own; shown once a tool has two accounts, or some are picked). It then lists and finds sessions only there, and in another one when you ask for that one; with none picked, it looks in all of them. The change says so in the conversation, with **Undo**. Togle reads the files those tools write as they go, never their sign-ins, and a work session never goes with a shared agent.
- **Chats on claude.ai and ChatGPT:** neither lets another app into your chats, so they come from the export of them each gives you: on claude.ai, Settings › Privacy › Export data; on ChatGPT, Settings › Data controls › Export data. Each emails you a link to a zip. Once it's downloaded, Togle finds it in your Downloads folder (or drop the zip, the folder you unpacked it to, or its `conversations.json` in the window, or pick it with **Choose an export…** at the bottom of that list). From then on its chats are listed with your work sessions, newest first, under the account they're from ("ChatGPT", or "ChatGPT (me@acme.com)" beside another account's export), and picked or asked for in words ("add my ChatGPT chat about the deploy checklist") the same way. A chat goes in under its name in your list of chats (one with no name yet is named by what it's about, once studied). What goes in: what was said, on the branch the chat ended on (not the replies you asked it to try again, or the prompts you edited); what you attached that claude.ai kept the text of (up to 4,000 characters each); what the AI wrote you in an artifact or a canvas (up to 3,000); what a connector of yours gave it (1,500); a line for what it did (searched the web, ran code, made a picture). Its thinking, your custom instructions, its memory of you and what its own tools printed stay out, and keys and passwords are taken out. A chat is as of your last export: export again, and the chats you gave agents are read again from the newer one before your next call. Only the newest export of each account is read (a big claude.ai export's batches are one export), and of it only the files of conversations, never the pictures; a chat read from a newer export is never read back from an older one left behind, and one chat that can't be read doesn't lose the rest. An export unpacked a folder down in Downloads is found, the files of an unpacked one dropped in together go in as the export (its `chat.html` and `user.json` don't become documents), and one you gave on a drive that's out for now is kept for when it's back. Togle doesn't copy the export, and remembers only where one you gave it from elsewhere is. A company's account may not let you export: ChatGPT Business and Enterprise don't, and on Claude Team and Enterprise an owner decides. A chat from one of those you paste in, and it goes in as a document. A Claude Code session in the cloud (claude.ai/code) comes to this PC when you open it in your terminal (**Open in › Terminal** there, then run that in the project's folder): Claude Code keeps its own copy of it here, listed with the others. Codex's cloud tasks bring only their changes to this PC (`codex apply`), not what was said, so they aren't listed.
- **Skills:** how it handles one kind of situation in a call (pushback, a type of question, a part of the call): a name, when it applies, and how. While they fit (about 12,000 characters) every skill goes into each request, so following one costs no lookup mid-call; past that, each answer gets the two that fit the moment best, in full. The conversation and the call review improve a skill by its name, and each counts how often a call needed it.
- **Approved answers:** "when asked X, answer Y". They outrank the documents.
- **Memory:** facts it relies on in every call. Every fact goes into every answer, so memory has a budget of 4,000 characters, and a fact is short (under 300): near it, the conversation and the call review merge related facts and drop outdated ones before adding more, and the review says which facts had no room, to merge or forget others for.
- **Past calls:** what it remembers of each reviewed call: its brief, who it was with, a summary of what they wanted, what was decided and promised, facts about them, and what they asked with what you answered.
- **Code on this PC:** a project folder attached with the paperclip (**A project's code…**); see Pair programming above.
- **Its setup:** name, what it's for, instructions, About me, words to recognize, and what it does after each call (learn on its own, the default, as Hermes does; ask first; or review a call only when asked, with **Learn from this call**), all by telling it. Its own model for deep answers, if one was set before, can be put back to Settings' from its menu; **Share** and **Delete** are there too.

**The call review.** When you stop listening after a real conversation (2 or more new lines), the review runs on its own (unless the agent is set not to review calls), and the agent tells you in the conversation what it learned, the way Hermes says "Memory updated": one thing, in its line with **Undo**; more, in a line ("I learned 3 things from this call") with **Show** for a line each, each with **Undo**, and **Undo all**. By default it keeps what it found at once, facts as memory has room for them (those without it are named, to make room for). What would change what it knows, or says an amount or a deal heard on the call, it asks about (see How it gets better), and a call that taught it comes with an offer to keep its notes. With nothing new, it says so. Set to **Ask me first**, it asks about all it found, each tagged (Answer, Fact, Skill, Changed, Forget): facts that changed (old → new) or are no longer true, new facts, question/answer pairs, skills. Nothing is kept until you say; then it says what it kept, with Undo. What it keeps goes to the agent the review was for, in that agent's conversation, also when you've picked another one meanwhile. A review that fails (no connection, the plan's limit) leaves the call as it was, to review again (**Try again**); asking again while one runs doesn't start a second. The review reads its reply however the model formats it (as a list, numbered or in bold), and offers each fact and skill once. It also writes the call's summary and the facts about the people on it; when summaries of past calls are on, those are kept with the call (it says "I kept a summary of this call"), and reviewing the same call again updates what was kept. **Learn from this call**, under a call, starts the review at any time. Once it kept something, **See how I'd answer now** shows what changed: the call's last three questions asked again, each as it stood when it was asked (what had been said and shown by then, the screenshot taken for it, the brief, and what you'd told the agent by then), each once, with what the agent answered then as a line and its quick answer now. The running notes go with it only if they don't reach past the question (they'd tell it what came after, your answer too); otherwise it reads more of the call before the question instead. It keeps going while Settings or Agents is open.

Weird things that never reach the model were tried too (`npm test` has each), and what they broke is fixed:
- **On a call:** a model reply with no text says "No answer came back. Try again." instead of leaving an empty card. A pasted log of more than 60,000 characters is asked with its start and its end, and its card's title is its first 300 characters, on one line. The PC's clock set back an hour mid-call, or after New call, keeps the call's times in order.
- **Files an agent is taught with:** each is read in a worker of its own, with a memory and a time limit, so a file built to blow up is refused with why while calls go on: a 150 KB Word or PowerPoint file that unpacks to 50 MB of tiny paragraphs in 0.8–3 seconds, a PDF listing one page 20,000 times in 1.4, and a 1 MB PDF whose page unpacks to 300 MB in 28.5, with the main thread held up at most 34 ms. A page of 400 KB of "<", a comment or a script never closed, or a slide of 800 KB of paragraphs never closed reads in milliseconds, where the patterns that read them took 22 and 13 seconds (and four times that for each doubling). A document holds up to 5 million characters and an agent 20 million, a folder brings in its first 500 files and says there were more, and a long line (a paragraph of Chinese, a minified file) is searched whole, not just its start. Text that isn't Unicode is read in the code page it was saved in: this PC's (as Windows says), unless its words say another. Of 32 texts in 25 languages saved in Western, Central European and Cyrillic code pages, read on PCs of five code pages, 158 of the 160 come back exactly, where 50 did; a Croatian CSV from Excel reads right on an English PC. Chinese, Japanese, Korean and Thai are searched by pairs of characters, so a word inside a sentence is found. A crawl loads at most three pages for each page asked for, ends after five failures in a row, ends a page whose code never finishes, and downloads nothing.
- **Agents and settings:** an agent id that is a path deletes nothing; a damaged agent file loads what it can; a settings file edited by hand keeps the defaults for what isn't right, and the hubs you added.

How answers use the training:

| Knowledge size | What the model gets |
| --- | --- |
| Up to ~40k characters | Every document in full, numbered for citation. **What the cache needs.** Measured on the ChatGPT backend: a prompt start is read from the cache only once about 2,048 tokens of it are shared with earlier requests (as of 2026-10-23; OpenAI's API documents 1,024), and then from the second or third request that shares it. A sales agent with four documents has a setup of about 1,800 tokens, so its answers early in a call were never cached. With the quick answer's instructions (the same for every request) moved ahead of the call, in the spoken-call test quick answers 2–4 read about 88% of their prompt from the cache (2,048–2,176 of 2,294–2,397 tokens), where none did before, and the 18 test calls answered as well as before. Longer calls, where the call so far is long, are cached either way. `npm run perf` shows the share for your calls.

## Setup

Requires Windows 10/11, macOS 14.2+ or Ubuntu 22.04+ (with PulseAudio, or PipeWire's pulse server), and Node 22.12+. Linux execution remains unverified. Mac build commands, permissions, feature differences and the manual acceptance checklist are in [docs/macos.md](docs/macos.md).

```sh
npm ci
npm run dev             # then Settings → Speech to text → Install
```

On Windows, `npm run setup:whisper` sets up a development copy in `resources/whisper` instead (~1.9 GB with an NVIDIA GPU), which Togle runs while nothing is installed from Settings. It uses the CUDA build and `large-v3-turbo` when it finds an NVIDIA GPU, otherwise the CPU build and `base`. You can override both: `npm run setup:whisper -- --cpu --model ggml-small.bin`. It also downloads Parakeet TDT v3 (~670 MB, skip with `--no-parakeet`; Whisper then does everything). Interrupted downloads resume where they stopped.

Then choose **Continue with ChatGPT** (the window's first card, or Settings, the gear), and press **Start call** when the call begins. Headphones work best.

**Claude with your subscription** (Settings → **AI for answers** → **Claude** → **Use Claude with: Claude subscription**). Choose **Sign in with Claude**, then finish the official Claude Code sign-in in your browser. An existing Claude Code sign-in is recognized automatically. Togle uses the official `@anthropic-ai/claude-agent-sdk` and its bundled, unmodified Claude Code executable, the same CLI-backed route as [Hermes's subscription plugin](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-official-plugins/tree/main/claude-subscription-directsdk). It never reads, copies or refreshes Claude Code's credentials. The sign-in is shared with Claude Code on this PC, so switching accounts or signing out here affects it too.

Your Claude plan's limits, usage-credit settings and organization policy apply. A successful sign-in does not override an organization that has disabled subscription access: its error is shown. There is no automatic fallback to API-key billing. Subscription models and efforts have their own choices (the CLI's `opus`, `sonnet` and `haiku` aliases), separate from API-key models. Answers stream, frames go as images, and Togle's knowledge, code-reading and training tools run through an in-process MCP server. The SDK's built-in file and shell tools, other configured MCP servers, project settings and session transcripts are disabled; web search is enabled only for deep answers when selected. Speech recognition is a separate choice below.

**Claude with your API key** (Settings → **AI for answers** → **Claude** → **Use Claude with: Anthropic API key**). Paste a key from console.anthropic.com; it's kept encrypted like the ChatGPT sign-in. It goes through Anthropic's official SDK (`@anthropic-ai/sdk`) over the same Chromium network stack as ChatGPT. Not yet measured on real calls:

- **A model and an effort per job.** By default it's Claude Opus 5.5 for every job. Its thinking is always on, and the effort decides how much it thinks: low where words must come quickly (quick answers, the screen, notes), high for deep answers. Any model your key can use can go to any job.
- **Caching.** The agent's setup (instructions, skills, documents) goes first with a cache breakpoint, so each job's requests after its first read it from Anthropic's cache: cheaper, and quicker to start. So does the call so far, up to its last 90 seconds (lines that may still change): Anthropic caches by block, so the call goes in blocks of 8 lines that repeat from one answer to the next, the newest of them marked, and the next answer reads up to there from the cache (about 4,100 tokens of a 240-line call that each quick answer processed again before). A request that may look something up also caches the conversation for its next round.
- **Web search for deep answers** (on by default, up to 3 searches per answer over all its rounds, billed per search; Train, which edits the agent, never searches). The card shows the search as it starts, and the pages the answer cites close it.
- **Retries.** A request that fails before any of its text reached the card (a dropped connection, Claude overloaded) is tried again once; after that, a retry would write the text twice. A quick answer that hits your key's rate limit says when to try again instead of waiting for it. The SDK's environment settings for other tools (`ANTHROPIC_BASE_URL`, `ANTHROPIC_CUSTOM_HEADERS`, `ANTHROPIC_AUTH_TOKEN`) don't apply: the key goes to api.anthropic.com only.
- **When Claude declines.** An answer Claude's safety checks decline is retried in the same request on the model Anthropic picks for the reason (`fallbacks: "default"`; Haiku has none).
- Knowledge search and reading code work as with ChatGPT. Errors say what to do (key turned down, rate limit, credit balance).

**An OpenAI-compatible endpoint** (Settings → AI model → **Other API / local**: OpenAI API, OpenRouter, Groq, Ollama, LM Studio). Knowledge search, reading code and Train go through the model's function calling, which most hosted models have; a local model needs tool support (in Ollama, one with the "tools" tag). A server that turns tools down for a model is asked without them from then on: answers come from what the prompt holds (the agent's documents are still searched on this PC for each question), and Train says the model can't change the agent. A prompt too long for the model is that error, not a model without tools. When a model looks something up after it was told to answer (Ollama doesn't take that instruction), it's asked again with no tools listed, so the card gets an answer instead of staying empty, and from then on its last round lists none. A reasoning model's thinking that comes inside the answer text (`<think>…</think>`, as DeepSeek R1 and Qwen 3 write it on servers that don't set it apart) is left out of the card.

## Sign in with ChatGPT

Togle uses OpenAI's [Sign in with ChatGPT for open-source apps](https://developers.openai.com/siwc/token-sharing-open-source), with ChatGPT plan usage: answers count against your ChatGPT plan (Plus or Pro), not an API bill. **Continue with ChatGPT** opens your browser. The first time, ChatGPT asks you to allow Togle to use your plan, and registers Togle for your account and this computer (a random host ID, nothing that identifies you). Later sign-ins reuse that registration.

- The sign-in uses PKCE and comes back to a loopback address on this PC. Togle checks the ID token against OpenAI's published keys, and checks that you allowed plan use, before any request.
- Answers go to the public Responses API (`api.openai.com/v1/responses`): never stored at OpenAI (`store: false`), streamed, and only with the fields that route accepts. Text and screenshots, no audio: speech is heard on this PC (see above).
- What Togle uses of your plan, and any limit you set for it, is in ChatGPT's settings: **Manage usage** in Togle opens [chatgpt.com/settings/usage](https://chatgpt.com/settings/usage). When a limit is reached, Togle stops answering on its own for a few minutes and says so; **Suggest** still tries.
- **Sign out** forgets the tokens here and ends the session at OpenAI. You can also disconnect Togle in ChatGPT's settings.

Tokens and API keys are encrypted with the OS keystore (Electron `safeStorage`: DPAPI on Windows, Keychain on macOS, libsecret on Linux) in Togle's user-data folder.

## Togle Microphone

[Togle Microphone](https://example.com/microphone/) is a separate app, not open source, that removes background noise from your voice in every call app, on your computer. Togle works the same without it.

- **Found by itself.** Togle Microphone leaves a link file in its own user-data folder and serves a local socket that only your user can reach. Togle reads the file, proves it with the token in it, and from then on knows whether the clean microphone is on and what it cleans. Only that state travels over the link, never audio. The protocol is in [`src/shared/microphone-link.ts`](src/shared/microphone-link.ts).
- **On for calls.** With **Turn on during calls** (Settings → Togle Microphone), a call that starts turns it on first, so the call hears the clean microphone, and turns it off again when the call ends, unless it was already on. The call strip shows **Clean mic** while it is. Dictation waits for the last words of its cleaned audio.
- **Signed in to it.** Togle Microphone is used with its own account. While you're signed out of it, a call leaves it alone, and Settings and the call strip offer **Sign in**, which opens its window. Togle only learns whether someone is signed in, never who.
- **What your microphone picks up.** Without it, Togle measures your microphone during calls, without noise suppression, while nobody speaks: how often the background is loud enough to hear, short sounds such as typing, and how far your voice is above the background. Only levels are kept, plus a few seconds of background to play back, in memory. After a noisy call (at most every few days, never during one), Togle shows what it measured, plays back what the microphone picked up, and links to Togle Microphone. **Check my microphone** in Settings measures it on request. **Show what my microphone picks up** turns all of it off; so does **Don't show this again** on the card.

The link to Togle Microphone's page uses `TOGLE_SITE` in [`src/shared/microphone-link.ts`](src/shared/microphone-link.ts), a placeholder (`https://example.com`) until the site has its domain.
